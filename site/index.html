Scheff for Zello

A Zello radio that fits in your pocket.

Firmware for ESP32 handhelds that signs in to your Zello account over Wi-Fi, joins a channel, and talks both ways through the board's own microphone and speaker. Text messages too. No phone, no extra hardware.

Install it from this page

Installing needs desktop Chrome or Edge (Web Serial). Installing needs a secure (https) page.

What it does

Push-to-talk
Hold the button on screen, or the board's own talk button. Zello is half duplex, so a press does nothing while someone else is talking. Prefer to tap once to start and again to stop? Turn on tap to talk.
Text messages
The channel's messages scroll in the activity panel, with a beep for each new one. Type and send your own from the device or the browser.
Screen or browser
Every setting is on the touch screen and on a built-in web page. Change it in one and the other follows.
Your channels
Favorites go to the top, then the channels you join most.
Screensaver
A schefflera drifts round the screen showing who is talking. Audio keeps playing, and a pocket can't wake it by accident.
Backup
One YAML file holds every setting and the channel list. Download it, or save it to a microSD card.
Crash reports
Crashes and dropped connections are written to microSD, so problems can be traced.

Supported devices

  • LilyGO T-Display-P4

    ESP32-P4, 568×1232 AMOLED

    Talk with the button on screen. BOOT is the screen button.

  • LilyGO T-Deck / T-Deck Plus

    ESP32-S3, keyboard and trackball

    Hold the trackball click to talk, or press Space to start and again to stop.

  • Seeed Wio Tracker L2

    ESP32-S3, touch screen

    Hold the GPIO0 button or tap Talk. Wake is the screen button.

  • Nicolai Electronics Tanmatsu

    ESP32-P4, full keyboard

    Listen and text only, as it has no microphone. Installing replaces the badge.team launcher.

Connect your Zello account

Each step works on the device's screen or in a browser, and each one follows the other.

  1. Install and power on

    Use the install button at the top of this page. On first boot the device starts an open Wi-Fi hotspot called Scheff-XXXX.

  2. Open the setup page

    Join the Scheff-XXXX hotspot from your phone or computer and go to http://192.168.4.1. Nothing pops up on its own, so type the address in.

  3. Put it on your Wi-Fi

    Pick your network from the scan or type its name, then enter the password. The device joins, saves it and restarts. From then on the setup page is at http://<device-ip>, and the address is shown at the bottom of every setup screen.

  4. Get a Zello developer token

    The public Zello network only lets third-party devices sign in with a developer token, a long code. Sign in at developers.zello.com with your Zello account and create one. Using Zello Work? Skip this step.

  5. Enter your account

    Fill in Zello account on the setup page:

    FieldZello (zello.io)Zello Work
    Username and passwordYour Zello loginYour Zello Work login
    Developer tokenPaste the whole tokenNot used
    Network nameNot usedYour network's subdomain

    Paste the token in the browser, as it is far too long to type on the device. Once you save, the device signs in straight away.

  6. Pick a channel

    Zello has no public channel directory, so add the channels you want by name. Names must match exactly, spaces and capitals included. Then hold the button and talk.

When the token expires

Development tokens expire, typically after 30 days. When Zello refuses the sign-in, the device shows the token's expiry date. Create a new token and paste it in the setup page. Leaving the field blank keeps the saved one.

Where your password goes

Your password and token stay on the device. The setup page never sends them back to the browser, only whether one is saved. A backup file does include them unencrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
